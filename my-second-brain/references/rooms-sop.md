# Room Guides -- Layer 2 SOP rooms

SOP rooms are named by INTENT (what the process achieves), never by department. Capturing an SOP = the owner talks through how it happens today (the honest version, not the ideal version); the AI writes it as a numbered SOP note from the `SOP.md` template (`function:`, `owner:`, `last_verified:` filled). The **only-the-boss-can-do** section is mandatory in every SOP capture; it is where the insight usually lives.

Universal question spine (adapt per room, one at a time):

1. Trigger: what starts this process? (an order lands, a hire signs, the month ends)
2. Walk it: what happens first? then? (let them talk; you structure)
3. Who does each step today, actually?
4. Which steps can ONLY one specific person do (usually the owner)? Why: authority, skill, or access?
5. What goes wrong most often, where?
6. How do you know it finished properly? (any check, or vibes)

Universal insight angles (every SOP room):

- **The only-you steps:** "N of these M steps only you can do." The reference classic. Name whether each is authority, skill, or access; access ones are the cheapest to fix.
- **The vibes checkpoint:** no definition of done; quality is whoever-did-it's mood that day.
- **The most-often-wrong step** is usually the least-documented one; that correlation is worth saying out loud.

Room-specific color below: the door sign, plus what to listen for.

---

## Staff-Onboarding

**Door sign:** from signed offer to independently productive.
Listen for: day-one chaos (no checklist, no accounts ready), shadowing-as-strategy, "they will pick it up". Extra question: "How long until a new hire stops needing to ask where things are?" Insight color: onboarding length is usually a proxy for how much of the operation lives in heads; captured SOPs shorten it mechanically.

## Client-Onboarding

**Door sign:** from deal-closed to client-successfully-started.
Listen for: the handoff cliff between whoever sold and whoever delivers; expectations set verbally in the sales conversation that delivery never hears about. Extra question: "What does the client receive in the first 48 hours after saying yes?" Insight color: most client disappointment is manufactured in this window, before delivery even starts.

## Sales-Followup

**Door sign:** from quotation sent (or interest shown) to closed or cleanly dead.
Listen for: no defined cadence, follow-up as guilt-driven improvisation, no such state as "cleanly dead" (deals rot instead of closing). Extra question: "After how many days of silence do you give up, and is that a decision or a drift?" Insight color: pair with the Sales room's silent-deal graveyard; here it becomes a writable cadence.

## Delivery

**Door sign:** from confirmed order to delivered-and-accepted.
Listen for: informal quality gates, the owner as final inspector of everything, customer-caused delays with no protocol. Extra question: "When delivery depends on the client doing something, what happens while you wait?" Insight color: the owner-as-final-inspector step caps throughput at the owner's calendar; that is a scaling ceiling written into the process.

## Collection

**Door sign:** from invoice issued to money in the bank.
Listen for: awkwardness-driven delay (nobody wants to be the one who chases), no escalation ladder, credit terms granted by memory. Extra questions: "Who sends the reminder, on what day, and what does message number three say?" / "Who decides a client can pay late, and where is that recorded?" Insight color: collection is usually a courage process wearing an admin costume; a written ladder removes the courage requirement.

## Complaint-Handling

**Door sign:** from complaint received to resolved-and-learned.
Listen for: every complaint escalating to the owner, no severity tiers, resolution without record. Extra question: "What is the most a staff member can offer an angry customer without calling you?" Insight color: the authority vacuum (also visible from the Customer-Service room) is fixable with one written table: tier, what can be offered, by whom.

## Content-Production

**Door sign:** from idea to published (or printed) piece.
Listen for: production tied to one person's inspiration, no repeatable pipeline for the thing that already worked once, approval bottleneck at the owner. Extra question: "The last piece that performed well: could anyone else in the company reproduce the steps that made it?" Insight color: pairs with Content-Assets' one-hit wonder; the SOP is how a hit becomes a series.

## Purchasing

**Door sign:** from need-noticed to goods-received-and-checked.
Listen for: reorder by eyeball, one person holding all supplier relationships, no receiving check (short deliveries discovered weeks later). Extra question: "How do you know it is time to reorder, and how do you know what arrived is what you paid for?" Insight color: the eyeball threshold works until the person with the eyeballs takes leave; a written reorder point is the cheapest resilience the business can buy.

## Payroll-Monthly-Closing

**Door sign:** from month-end to everyone-paid-and-books-closed.
Listen for: the monthly miracle (one person, no documentation, statutory deadlines from memory), claims and OT arriving as WhatsApp archaeology. Extra question: "If the person who runs payroll is hospitalized on the 25th, what happens on the 28th?" Insight color: this process has real deadlines with real penalties; it is usually the single most under-documented high-stakes process in the company.

# Preset SOP rooms (created by toggles, or proposed later)

## Store-Open-Close (outlets toggle)

**Door sign:** the daily opening and closing ritual of a physical location.
Listen for: opening knowledge held by whoever "always opens", closing cash counts by trust, alarm/keys custody undefined. Extra question: "If both your usual openers are sick on the same morning, can anyone else open using only what is written down?" Insight color: this is the purest test of documentation-vs-heads in the whole business; it is also the easiest full SOP to capture in one sitting.

## Kitchen-Prep (F&B, propose when the business turns out to be F&B)

**Door sign:** daily prep from raw ingredients to service-ready.
Listen for: recipes in heads, portioning by feel, prep quantities guessed daily. Extra question: "If your kitchen lead resigned today, which dishes leave the menu with them?" Insight color: recipe-in-head is inventory risk, consistency risk, and succession risk wearing an apron; note that recipe SPECS live in Products-Services, the prep PROCESS lives here.

## Food-Safety (F&B, propose alongside Kitchen-Prep)

**Door sign:** the hygiene and safety routine: temperatures, cleaning schedules, certs.
Listen for: compliance as inspection-day theater, logs backfilled, cert expiries unknown. Extra question: "What happens in the hour after a customer reports food poisoning?" Insight color: the incident-response gap matters more than the daily log; one written page beats a year of backfilled temperature sheets. (Actual temperature ROWS stay in their logbook or tool; the vault holds the procedure and exceptions.)

## Importing (importing toggle)

**Door sign:** from overseas PO to goods cleared and in your warehouse. One shipment, one note is the working convention.
Listen for: the forwarder as an unwritten dependency, duty/permit knowledge held by one agent, shipment status tracked in WhatsApp. Extra question: "Where would a new staff member look to answer: where is our container right now?" Insight color: per-shipment notes turn WhatsApp archaeology into a paper trail; the forwarder relationship is often a bigger single point of failure than any supplier.

## Stock-Count (importing toggle)

**Door sign:** the counting ritual: cadence, method, variance handling.
Listen for: counts that happen "when it feels off", variance absorbed silently, no cutoff discipline. Extra question: "Last count: how big was the gap between the sheet and the shelf, and what happened because of it?" Insight color: variance without investigation is a standing invitation; the count SOP is less about counting and more about what a discrepancy triggers.

## Maintenance (equipment toggle)

**Door sign:** keeping the machines alive: schedules, checks, who-calls-whom on breakdown.
Listen for: run-to-failure as unofficial policy, the one technician's number in one phone, warranty terms never consulted. Extra question: "When the critical machine stops mid-shift, what are the first three phone calls, in order?" Insight color: the breakdown call-tree is the SOP that pays for the whole room the first time it is needed; pairs with the Equipment room's service log.
