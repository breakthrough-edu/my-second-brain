# Room Guides: Layer 1 function rooms

Function rooms are home addresses for each function's materials, logs, and reports. They are NOT departments and they never hold procedures (how-to lives in Layer 2, iron law). Each has an `Action-Log.md`; decisions go to the central Decisions room. Same capture rules as everywhere: one question at a time, 3 to 5 items is a full first capture, rows iron law applies hard in these rooms (reports and summaries in, transaction streams out).

---

## Marketing

**Door sign:** marketing materials, channel notes, reports, platform account states. Not the content assets themselves (those have their own room) and not how content gets made (SOP).
**Filing test:** used by marketing -> here; a reusable brand/content asset -> `Content-Assets/`; a platform's contract or commission terms -> `Vendors/`.

**Questions:**
1. Where do customers actually hear about you today? (name the real channels, not the aspirational ones)
2. Which channel do you spend money on, and roughly how much a month?
3. Which channel do you spend TIME on, and whose time?
4. Is there any report or number you check? (ads manager screenshot monthly? nothing? "nothing" is a capturable fact)
5. Any account states worth recording? (FB page admin access, who holds it; a penalized platform account; a dormant account with followers)

**Insight angles:**
- Spend vs attention mismatch: money on channel A, time on channel B, belief in channel C.
- Access risk: the ad account or page admin sits under one person's personal login (sometimes an ex-staffer or an agency).
- Measurement void: spending monthly with no number ever checked; the first monthly snapshot this room now invites is the fix's seed.

## Sales

**Door sign:** pipeline (one open deal, one note under `Pipeline/`), outward price lists, quotation templates, sales reports.
**Filing test:** chasing a deal not yet closed -> `Pipeline/` here; the moment it closes -> promote to `Clients/` + an engagement folder; pricing source of truth stays in `Products-Services/`.

**Questions:**
1. Right now, how many deals are you actively chasing? Name the top three.
2. For the biggest one: how did it come in, what stage is it at, what is the next step, and whose move is it?
3. How does a quotation get made today? (a template? Excel? each time from scratch?) Where does the sent copy live?
4. When a deal goes quiet, what happens? (honest answer: usually nothing; that is a capture, not a confession)
5. Do you know your close rate, even roughly? (a guess marked as a guess is fine)

**Insight angles:**
- The silent-deal graveyard: no follow-up mechanism for stalled deals; deals die of silence, not rejection.
- Whose-move blindness: for N of the named deals, the next step is actually waiting on the owner themselves.
- Close-rate folklore: the number is a feeling; even a month of pipeline notes makes it a number.

## Customer-Service

**Door sign:** complaint records, review/rating summaries, retention actions, service scripts' materials (the handling PROCESS lives in SOP `Complaint-Handling/`).
**Filing test:** the handling action or record -> here; the standard procedure -> SOP; a pattern worth a rule -> flag `#lesson-candidate` for distill.

**Questions:**
1. Where do complaints arrive? (WhatsApp, Google reviews, walk-in shouting)
2. Think of the last complaint that actually stung. What happened, and how was it resolved?
3. Is there a complaint that keeps coming back in different costumes? (same root, new customer)
4. Who handles a complaint when you are unreachable, and what are they allowed to give away? (refund authority, replacement authority)
5. Any wins worth recording: a furious customer turned regular?

**Insight angles:**
- The repeat complaint: it recurs because the fix each time is service recovery, never process change; that is a Layer 2 gap wearing a Layer 1 costume.
- Authority vacuum: nobody but the owner can authorize a refund; every escalation is really a "find the boss" process.
- Commented-out feedback: complaints resolved in WhatsApp vanish; tonight's notes are the first pattern-detectable record.

## Operations

**Door sign:** the running of the operation: rosters, opening checklists' filled records, cross-SKU lists, the `IT-Systems/` sub-room (account inventory, system notes; passwords stay in a password manager, pointers only).
**Filing test:** keeps the operation running day to day -> here; employment lifecycle -> HR/Employees; how a process is done -> SOP.

**Questions:**
1. Walk me through a normal day: what has to happen for the business to run, from open to close?
2. Which of those things break when a specific person is absent?
3. What systems and accounts does the business run on? (POS, accounting software, Google Workspace, the domain name...) Who holds each login?
4. Where do the passwords live? (if the answer is "my head" or "a notebook", capture the fact and recommend a password manager; do NOT capture the passwords)
5. Any recurring operational fire that everyone treats as weather? ("the Tuesday delivery is always late")

**Insight angles:**
- The absence map: listing which daily operations break per absent person is usually the owner's first sight of their true bus factor.
- Account custody: the domain name or POS admin registered under someone's personal email (or an ex-vendor's).
- Weather that is actually a leak: the recurring fire nobody costed because it arrives weekly and small.

## HR

**Door sign:** policies, JDs, handbook materials, payroll matters (function ownership; statutory payment proofs point to Finance). People themselves live in `Employees/`.
**Filing test:** about a specific person -> their note in `Employees/`; about people-rules in general -> here; how onboarding runs -> SOP `Staff-Onboarding/`.

**Questions:**
1. Do written rules exist anywhere? (leave, lateness, claims; "in my head" and "we follow feel" are honest captures)
2. How does leave actually get requested and approved today?
3. Payroll: who runs it, on what tool, and what day of the month does it hurt?
4. When someone new joins, what exists to hand them? (anything written, or person-shadowing only)
5. What gets people in trouble here, and do they know it in advance?

**Insight angles:**
- Rules by folklore: policy exists only as precedent memory; every dispute is relitigated from scratch.
- Payroll single-thread: one person, one tool, no documented steps, every month a small miracle (feeds SOP `Payroll-Monthly-Closing/`).
- Onboarding by osmosis: new hires learn by shadowing whoever is least busy; consistency is luck.

## Finance

**Door sign:** statements, tax filings' pointers, banking facts, monthly snapshots, loan/instalment facts. Rows iron law applies hardest here: the vault holds pointers and monthly summaries, never the transaction stream.
**Filing test:** a report or summary -> here; the transaction stream -> stays in the accounting tool/bank; a money DECISION -> central Decisions room.

**Questions:**
1. Where do the numbers live today? (accounting software, an Excel, the accountant's office, a drawer of receipts)
2. When did you last see a P&L, and did you believe it?
3. What are the standing monthly outflows, top of head? (rent, payroll, the big supplier; rough is fine)
4. Any loans, instalments, or credit facilities? (lender, rough balance, monthly hit; pointers, not statements)
5. Who besides you can see the money picture? (accountant, spouse, nobody)
6. Is there a number you wish you knew every Monday morning?

**Insight angles:**
- The believability gap: books exist but the owner does not trust them; decisions run on the bank-balance-glance method.
- Snapshot void: no monthly ritual exists; the monthly snapshot this room invites is the smallest fix with the biggest sight gain.
- The Monday number: whatever the owner wished they knew weekly is a design brief, in their own words, for what this room should eventually surface. Capture it verbatim.
