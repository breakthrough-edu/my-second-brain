# Candidate: write natively in your market's language, do not translate

**The candidate rule:** content for a given language market should originate *in* that language, not be written in another language and translated. Translated copy carries the source language's sentence rhythm and idiom, which native readers feel as slightly off even when every word is correct. Draft in the target language from the start.

**Why it might hold:** fluency and trust ride on rhythm and idiom, not just accuracy. A translated sentence can be right and still read foreign, and foreign-reading marketing leaks credibility.

**Why it might not (for you):** if your market is single-language, this is moot. If your audience is itself bilingual and code-switches, a lightly mixed register may read *more* native than a purist one.

**How the loop tests it:** when natively-drafted pieces calibrate better with native reviewers and pull better in-market than translated ones, log confirmations. Note where your audience's actual code-switching habits change the answer.

**Confirmations:** 0 (seed hypothesis). Portable craft, not yet your proven rule.

> The specific language and any specific native-writing principles that one business uses are its own identity and were left out. What ships is the general discipline: originate in your market's language.
