# Marketing Doctrine (seed)

> This is a **seed**, not the answer. It carries only marketing *mechanism*: the few things that are true no matter what business runs them. Everything that a smart business should tune to its own market lives as a default in `thresholds.md` or as a candidate in `rubric/`, not here. Everything that was one particular business's identity was left out on purpose. The loop grows this file into your marketing's real doctrine over the coming months; do not treat these lines as settled truth.

## Hardened mechanism (the parts with no reasonable alternative)

- **The market is the judge, not taste.** Whether a piece worked is decided by how the market responds, not by how much the owner or the team liked it. Which market signal counts, and at what threshold, is yours to set (`thresholds.md`), but that the market decides, not preference, is fixed.
- **Audience calibration is a veto, never a green light.** A native reviewer or a trusted insider can *kill* a piece for sounding wrong, but their approval is not what releases it; the market signal is. Calibration filters out the clearly broken; it does not certify success.
- **Quality is enforced before publishing, not patched after.** There is a gate the work passes before it ships (a quality bar and a mechanical consistency sweep), rather than fixing things once they are already live. What the bar and the sweep check is yours to define (see `rubric/` and your `Brand-Style-Guide`); that there *is* a pre-publish gate is fixed.
- **Produce against your brand foundation, not from a blank slate.** Before producing customer-facing copy, read the brand foundation (`01_Assets/Brand-Strategy/` and `Target-Audience/`). Marketing with an empty brand foundation is generic by construction; the skill runs in a degraded, honest mode and says so.
- **Never present invented personas as real customer testimony.** Fictional avatars or example characters are fine as internal reference for drafting; they must never appear in customer-facing testimonials, case studies, or paid claims as if they were real people. This is an integrity mechanism, not a style choice.
- **Overrides are signal-driven, never gut-driven.** A kill/scale threshold can be overridden, but only against a logged signal and a reason, with a review date (`thresholds.md` override discipline). "It felt like it was working" and "we did not want to kill something we liked" are not valid overrides.

## Anti-patterns (mechanism-level, safe to hold generically)

- Treating a review pass or an internal calibration as the release gate. The market signal releases; calibration only vetoes.
- Rebuilding heavy multi-gate apparatus that slows production without improving the market signal. Keep the gate light and pre-write.
- Fixing quality after publishing instead of before.

## What is deliberately NOT here

Specific quality frameworks, channel lists, metric definitions, threshold numbers, positioning bets, content-pillar taxonomies, visual systems, voice registers, punctuation rules, and named avatars are all either tunable defaults (see `thresholds.md`, `rubric/`, `marketing-profile.md`) or your business's own identity (see your brand foundation). They are not doctrine. A seed that hardened those would be constraining you, not helping you.
